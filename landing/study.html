The Teacher

Samjhao, phir poocho.

Reading an explanation feels like learning and usually is not. This room explains, and then asks you questions back — which is the part that decides whether any of it stayed.

Send the page itself

A photo of the chapter, a screenshot, or a PDF. You do not have to type the question out, and you do not have to know what the topic is called before you can ask about it — which is usually the problem when you are stuck.

It asks where you are first

Before explaining anything large it finds out what you already know — which class, or simply whether you want the basic idea or the deeper one. Then one idea, one example you already understand, and one question back.

"Still samajh nahi aaya"

A good teacher does not repeat themselves louder. Say you are still lost and the next reply is a question, not another explanation — naming the two or three places this particular thing usually breaks and asking which one it is. Only then does it explain, and only that part.

Then it tests you

Ask to be quizzed and the questions come with options you answer by tapping, marked as you go. Ask for notes and you get notes — headings, steps, a table where a table is the honest answer, and a diagram when the thing is actually shaped like one.

It says when it does not know

An explanation delivered confidently is not the same as a correct one, and a student has no way to tell the difference. This room is told to say plainly when something is genuinely uncertain rather than producing a fluent answer anyway. For anything that will be marked, check it against your textbook.

Ek chapter leke dekho.

Photo bhejo, samjho, phir khud se test karwao. Free hai.
